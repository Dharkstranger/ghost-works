# Ghost works: what it is and who it is for

**One line:** a shared book between an artist and a gallery for every work on
consignment: where it is now, photos of its condition at each handover, sold
or not, and whether the artist's share was paid. Nothing goes missing,
nothing haunts either side.

**Who:** an artist in Lagos who leaves works with galleries, and the gallery
that holds and sells them. Both on a phone, often on slow data.

**The problem (sourced):** artists are taking galleries to court over
consigned works that were sold and never paid for (Urgent Matter's lawsuit
tracker, urgentmatter.press/tracking-art-lawsuits-legal-disputes-urgent-matter-database/,
2025 to 2026). Consignment is often agreed by word of mouth, with no shared
record of where a work is or what condition it was in.

**The idea (Kiroween, Costume Contest):** a work nobody can account for
becomes a **ghost**: it fades and drifts on screen until someone gives it a
handover, a photo, or a payment. The spookiness is the job: the scarier the
screen, the more is unaccounted for. When every work is solid, the book is
calm.

**It ends in Galleris.** "Put these works on Galleris" exports the works in
the columns the Galleris studio's spreadsheet import reads, so a gallery can
show them online. Ghost works itself stays free and simple; it never keeps
provenance beyond its handovers.

**Measured by:** works with no ghost (every work accounted for), and how many
galleries press "Put these works on Galleris".
